# Chunks

## What a chunk is
A chunk is a small piece of Japanese halfway between a phrase and a piece of grammar. Examples:

| Chunk | Means |
|---|---|
| どっちかというと | if anything, I'd say more... |
| というわけで | so, with that said |
| みたいな感じで | kind of like... |
| そりゃそう | well yeah, obviously |
| ～というのは | as for..., the thing about... (学校というのは…) |

They're not quite idioms, they're not full fixed phrases, and they don't translate one-for-one. They fill the gaps basic grammar plus words can't express.

**Why they matter:** Harry treats chunks as being as important as grammar. Japanese is basically a few hundred chunks with the rest of your words slotted into them. The more chunks a learner knows, understands and tries to use, the more Japanese they sound.

## Finding them in a video
Read the transcript and look for multi-word pieces that:
- **repeat** (the creator says them more than once, or they're common in speech generally)
- **carry meaning beyond their parts** (って感じ, わけじゃない, ってことは, じゃないですか used to share an assumption, ～しかない, ～てみる, ～ことにする)
- **link or frame ideas** (というか, とりあえず…から, っていう, ってことで, そういう意味で)
- **react** (マジか, なるほどね, そりゃそうだ, さすがに)

**Not chunks:**
- **A single word:** that's a word card.
- **A full sentence or proverb:** set phrases and proverbs only sparingly.
- **Bare grammar Tae Kim covers on its own:** a lone て-form, or ない. A chunk is a usable piece people reach for when they talk.

## How many
Most 15-minute videos have about 5-15 chunks worth a card. Give each its own card (`"kind": "chunk"`), with a sentence that shows how it's used and a note on what it does. Skip chunks already on the learner's known list (`known_chunks_heard` in `unknown.json`).
