# The method on one page

## The big picture: base → input → output
You build a **base**:
- kana
- a little kanji if you need it
- one read of grammar
- the first 500 words

Then you build a **mental model** of Japanese through massive listening. Speaking grows out of that model. Each part caps the next: you can't understand what you don't have the words for, and you can't say what you've never understood.

## The core ideas
1. **All the time you set aside for immersion is listening.** Swap leisure reading (a book on the train) for Japanese listening. Anki already gives you plenty of reading.
2. **Three kinds of listening:**
   - **passive:** every second you reasonably can, with earphones in
   - **the one-video grind:** one ~15-minute video learned completely
   - **enjoyable active:** sit-down watching of whatever holds your attention; switch the moment you're bored
3. **Active listening starts on day one.** It's less understandable at first, and you do it anyway. Before about 500 words, a Japanese-only Instagram feed is the easiest way in.
4. **Anki is required.** Pace new cards by retention, not a target. Go hard to about 4,000-5,000 words in 3-6 months without dropping listening, then slow right down and put the time into listening.
5. **Make your own cards from what you watch,** with Claude. The only ready-made decks:
   - the Hikaru 500
   - the optional 300-kanji deck
   - the JLPT deck, for people sitting the test
6. **The X factor: do everything in Japanese.** Phone language, feeds, music, leisure. Every bit of your normal life you can switch to Japanese is free immersion.
7. **Rules plant seeds, your ear does the learning.** Grammar rules, pitch rules and the kanji deck only tell you what to listen for. The real learning is hearing and mimicking real Japanese until it's part of you.
8. **Chunks** (どっちかというと, というわけで, みたいな感じで…) are as important as grammar. They're the building blocks of sounding Japanese.
9. **Your head is the training ground for speaking,** from day one. Real conversation is perfect immersion.
10. **Copy one person** (your "language parent") for how you want to sound.

## Daily time
- **There's no minimum.** Less just means slower.
- **About 2 hours of active listening a day is the ideal minimum** for "fairly fluent in about a year". Below that, progress gets dramatically slower.
- **Busy people (job, kids, a business) can usually get at least an hour** in 15-30 minute blocks, plus passive listening in every gap: commute, dishes, gym, walking.
- **Truly only one sit-down hour?** Get up 30 minutes earlier and find the 10-15 minute pockets first. If it really is one hour, it goes on Anki, and your listening is your current video on passive repeat in your earphones all day. Listening can't drop to zero.
- **Realistic timeline:** fairly fluent in about a year at 2+ hours of active a day; a high level often takes 2-4 years. Harry's N1 in nine months was his own extreme result.

## Day one (the first 24 hours)
Everyone:
1. Switch your phone to Japanese.
2. Set up a Japanese-only Instagram and scroll it for 30-60 minutes. You're not meant to understand it yet.
3. Log it in the JIL Tracker.
4. Post an intro in the Skool, with any questions you have.

Then:
- **Can't read kana yet:** start hiragana in a notebook from a chart. Anki starts once kana is done, in about 1-2 weeks.
- **Already read kana:** install Anki and do your first 10 cards of the Hikaru 500.

**Studied Japanese before?** Everyone takes the same path, you just move faster through the base:
- Skip kana if you read it, and skip the 300-kanji deck.
- Skim Tae Kim fast for anything you haven't met yet.
- Go through the Hikaru 500 and delete every card where you know every word and understand the whole sentence. You're left with only the cards that matter to you.
- Then make your decks with the deck-maker like everyone else. Your known-words list starts with every Hikaru 500 word and picks up the words in any deck already in your Anki (an old Core deck counts). If a generated card teaches you nothing new, delete it the same way.

## The stages, and when to move on
| Stage | What you're doing | You're ready to move on when... |
|---|---|---|
| 0 Start Here | Set up, understand the method, first session done | Your first week's checklist is done |
| 1 The Base | Kana (1-2 weeks). The 300-kanji deck if you need it. Read Tae Kim once. The Hikaru 500. Daily Anki and listening. Talking in your head. | The Hikaru 500 is done, you've given Tae Kim one quick read, and the kanji deck is done if you needed it |
| 2 Your Niche | One creator and the one-video method. Your own cards from your videos (words and chunks). First real conversations. Pitch by ear. | **About 4,000 words.** That's when you switch to Japanese definitions, and your main creator should be mostly followable. |
| 3 Intermediate → Advanced | Japanese definitions, a language parent, feedback on your speaking, politeness, patching holes in your vocabulary | **About 10,000 words:** you understand most everyday YouTubers who aren't on specialist topics |
| 4 Advanced | Complex listening, dense reading for rare words, closing gaps in specialist topics | The finish line (below). There's no final gate: you pick a new niche and keep going. |

**Rough timing:**
- Stage 1: about 1-2 months.
- The Anki push to 4,000-5,000 words runs across your first 3-6 months, from the Hikaru 500 into your niche, so it covers Stages 1 and 2.
- After that it depends on your hours.

**The finish line:**
- you understand native content in your niches without subtitles
- you hold natural conversations
- you can switch to polite Japanese when it counts

After that there's no end point. Pick a new niche, learn its words first, then immerse in it. Business Japanese and handwriting kanji are optional extras for people who need them for work.

**Not the goal:** passing as Japanese, or perfect pitch accent.
