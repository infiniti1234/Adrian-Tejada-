# Harry's story and numbers

Use these when an answer needs proof from his own experience. Always say "Harry", never "I". Don't add anything that isn't here.

- **Background:** Australian. Started Japanese at about 16, in October 2018. Never lived in Japan; he got fluent from Australia through YouTube, HelloTalk and gaming with Japanese friends.
- **N1:** passed JLPT N1 on his first attempt in July 2019, about nine months after starting. He aimed for 50-60 new words a day (some days only 20-30) and had roughly 8,000 words by then. He listened from waking up to going to sleep, slept far too little, and doesn't recommend copying that part. About 7,000 targeted words is enough for N1.
- **Words:** about 8,000 words by the N1 test, about 8,500 in Anki in that first push overall, and never much over 15,000 words in total. He still does daily Japanese reviews, and does short Anki pushes when he starts a new topic. He did this with business, marketing and finance.
- **Kanji:** he wrote about 2,500-3,000 kanji from memory with English-on-the-front cards, then gave that method up. Hours of reviews, failing about half. It's his "don't do this" story.
- **Speaking:** his biggest speaking jump came during COVID, gaming with Japanese friends for hours a day; often five to seven hours on calls and games. His level today means he's often mistaken for a Japanese person.
- **Talent:** his ear for copying accents probably helped his pronunciation, but his overall level came from the method: copying natural sources, volume and feedback.
- **Accent:** he speaks standard Japanese mixed with Kansai-ben, picked up purely from listening.
- **Politeness:** speaking very politely, even over the top, made strangers, shop staff and contacts like him far more.
- **Other languages:** he has Russian and Spanish decks (paused to focus on Japanese).
- **Comparing himself:** he measures himself against high-level native speakers, not other learners, and still finds things to fix after more than seven years.
