# The JLPT

## Do you even need it?
- **Ask why first.** Take it for a visa, a job, a school, or because you genuinely want the goal. If you don't need it, put the effort into your interests instead.
- **Below N2 it's pretty pointless:** basically a money-making scheme. There's no speaking or writing, so it doesn't show your real level.
- **Which level:** if a school only *prefers* N1 or N2, aim for N2. N1 realistically means studying non-stop. If N3 is all you need, don't worry; it's mostly normal Japanese.
- **It's not real Japanese.** Passing a test fast and getting good at real Japanese are two different goals with two different approaches.
  - **JLPT listening is easy:** clear, well-recorded single speakers.
  - **Real hard listening** is natives talking fast to each other.
- **What N1 covers:** about 10% of most niches. It's broad "corporate office" Japanese, not any one field. After passing, Harry still had no MMA or jiu-jitsu vocabulary.
- **Do the basics first,** whatever level you're aiming for: kana, the kanji deck if you need it, and the Hikaru 500. The process is the same for every level.

## How to prepare
- **Vocabulary is the only real barrier.**
  - There's no speaking or writing section.
  - If you know the words and can pick out the sounds, which listening topics you practised doesn't really matter.
- **The JIL JLPT deck:** 7,160 words in one deck (not split by level), built specifically for passing the test. About 7,000 targeted words is enough for N1. While you sprint, it **replaces** your niche cards: your new cards each day come from the JLPT deck instead (keep doing your reviews), and after the test you go back to cards from your own videos. Pace it by retention like any deck (start at 10 or more a day, as many as you can while retention holds at 75-80%), so the earlier you start before your test, the better.
- **What to practise:** JLPT-style material, not general YouTube. Past-paper listening plus reading. For the months before the test, past papers are your main practice.
- **Sleep:** sleep and eat properly before the test, and don't cut sleep while you prepare. Harry slept about 3 hours a night and fell asleep doing Anki on his phone. Don't copy that.
- **The format:** a few past papers show you the format. Refresh the exam timing if you've been out of school a while.
- **After the test,** for real Japanese your own niche words beat any JLPT list.

## How Harry passed N1 in nine months
Harry started Japanese in October 2018 at 16 and passed N1 on his first attempt in July 2019. The plan:
- **Words:** about 50-60 new words a day (some days only 20-30), reviews first, always pushing the number up. He had roughly 8,000 words after nine months.
- **The word list:** the official lists had been removed, so he built his own of about 7,000 words by level. Making cards by hand took hours a day; today the JLPT deck and Claude do that part.
- **Listening:** from waking up to going to sleep.
- **The test itself:** about two and a half hours in two long blocks with a break, all multiple choice. He walked out sure he'd failed after guessing between two answers again and again, and passed.

**The honest part:** it was before AI, it was messy, and he barely slept. Use it as proof of what volume does, not as a schedule to copy. The updated method (Claude-built decks from your own videos, the one-video method) gets you there with far less waste.
